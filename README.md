# Effects of the Contract Year on NBA Player Performance and Effort
## Overview
This research paper examined the effects of an NBA player being in the final year of their contract (known as the contract year) on their effort and performance. The paper primarily sought to answer the question of whether players intentionally increase their performance and effort in attempts to secure a new, more lucrative contract. Large datasets were created and regression was used to elucidate these effects. Foundational research from Berri et. al and Stiroh et. al. was built upon.
## Data
Performance data was measured with the RAPTOR index, a composite statistic encapsulating all aspects of player performance. Contract data, including player option status and contract expirations, were obtained through spotrac. Player effort was quantified using the "hustle index", a composite metric developed by perthirtysix, that uses niche statistics such as box-outs and charges drawn to evaluate overall effort.
## Conclusions
After running the regressions, using rigorous controls and data cleaning, no evidence was observed in the modern NBA of performance or effort manipulation in the contract year.
